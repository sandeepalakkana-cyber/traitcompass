About TraitCompass: Scientific Methodology & Clinical Disclaimers

Our mission is to translate complex psychometric and visual neuroscience research into accessible, private, interactive screening tools.

⚠️

Important Clinical & Medical Disclaimer

The assessments, questionnaires, and color vision tests on TraitCompass are engineered solely for informational, educational, and self-screening purposes. They do not constitute formal medical, ophthalmological, psychiatric, or diagnostic advice. Under no circumstances should results be used to substitute for a comprehensive evaluation by an optometrist, ophthalmologist, licensed clinical psychologist, or psychiatrist. If you are experiencing distress or sudden visual changes, please seek professional healthcare immediately.

1. The Ishihara Test: History & Construction

In 1917, Dr. Shinobu Ishihara, a professor at the University of Tokyo and military physician, published a revolutionary series of pseudo-isochromatic plates designed to identify color vision deficiency among recruits for the Imperial Japanese Army.

The brilliance of Ishihara’s technique lies in pseudo-isochromatism (meaning "false same-color"). By interspersing dots of varying sizes and luminance, brightness cues are neutralized. The figure and background are chosen from colors that lie directly along the dichromatic confusion lines in the CIE 1931 chromaticity diagram:

  • Protan confusion lines converge toward the copunctal point at approximately $(x=0.747, y=0.253)$.
  • Deutan confusion lines converge toward $(x=1.40, y=-0.40)$.
  • Tritan confusion lines converge toward $(x=0.171, y=0.00)$.

2. Digital Display Limitations vs. Printed Plates

While clinical optometrists rely on calibrated pigment-printed plates illuminated by standard CIE Illuminant C or D65 daylight simulators (6500K color temperature), our online test runs in standard web browsers. It is vital to recognize the inherent technical variances:

  • Screen Color Spaces: Modern displays operate primarily in sRGB, Display P3, or Adobe RGB. Differences in monitor gamut, backlight technology (OLED vs. IPS LCD), and panel aging introduce small spectral shifts.
  • Ambient Illumination: Warm tungsten lighting or glare can artificially alter perceived dot saturation.
  • Software Tinting: Operating system utilities such as Apple True Tone, Windows Night Light, and f.lux dynamically shift display white points, which can artificially trigger false positive results.

For these reasons, our test is labeled as a screening assessment rather than a definitive diagnostic test.

3. TraitCompass Behavioral Health Framework

Beyond visual screening, TraitCompass provides structured self-reflection tools covering behavioral traits, neurodivergent adaptations, and mood spectrums.

Our guides—including assessments for Social Anxiety Triggers, Antisocial Personality Traits (ASPD), and High-Functioning Depression—are synthesized from peer-reviewed psychiatric rating scales (such as the Liebowitz Social Anxiety Scale, Hare Psychopathy Checklist, and PHQ-9), stripped of stigmatizing colloquialisms and framed within modern cognitive-behavioral science.
